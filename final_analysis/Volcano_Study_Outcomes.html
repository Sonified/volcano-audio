Study Overview

What we did: Developed a web-based platform that transforms near-real-time volcanic seismic data into audible sound. Participants (N=6) listened to 24 hours of volcanic data, marked interesting features, and completed pre/post mood surveys plus awe and nature connectedness scales.

Hypotheses:

  • H1: Audification will reveal seismic features overlooked through visual analysis.
  • H2: Listening to volcanic data will improve affect and sense of connection to nature.

Study period: November-December 2025, conducted over three weeks. This study received IRB approval through the University of Oregon.

Volcanoes included: Great Sitkin, Shishaldin, Spurr (Alaska); Kilauea, Mauna Loa (Hawaii)

Experimental Design

  • N = 6 real participants (after excluding test accounts)
  • 17 complete sessions across 3 weeks (all with Pre+Post mood data)
  • Data source: USGS volcanic seismic station data from 5 active volcanoes
  • Measures:
    • Pre/Post mood (6 items, 1-5 scale) - administered immediately before and after each session
    • AWE-SF (12 items, 1-7 scale) - administered immediately after each session
    • CNS (14 items, 1-5 scale) - Pre at intake, Post before final session (not after completion)
    • Feature annotations - marked during listening sessions

Key Findings

Mood Item Pre Mean Post Mean Δ p-value Interpretation
Focused 3.06 3.47 +0.41 0.0041*** ✅ Significant increase
Calm 3.35 3.65 +0.29 0.0962 Trending positive
Connected 1.76 2.00 +0.24 0.1635 Trending positive
Nervous 1.59 1.41 -0.18 0.3818 Anxiety decreased (n.s.)
Wonder 1.88 1.94 +0.06 0.6684 Stable
Energized 3.06 2.71 -0.35 0.0826 Decreased

Volcano sonification significantly enhanced focus (p=0.0041, Δ=+0.41), a clear cognitive benefit measured immediately before and after each session. This represents increased concentration and attention during the listening experience.

Calm also showed a strong positive trend approaching significance (p=0.096, Δ=+0.29), suggesting the platform promotes relaxation alongside focus. Connected to nature improved (+0.24) and anxiety decreased (-0.18), though these changes were not statistically significant. The combination of increased focus with improved calm creates a "relaxed focus" state - enhanced concentration without tension or anxiety.

Measurement notes: Pre/Post mood surveys were administered immediately before and after each session, providing high-quality data on the intervention's direct impact. These are the most reliable measures in the study. Statistical significance tested using paired t-tests.

Nature Connectedness (CNS): Mixed Results

Participants with complete Pre + Post CNS data: 4 of 6 (P1, P2, P3, P4)

Participant Sessions Features CNS Pre CNS Post Δ
P1 5 114 52 42 -10
P2 4 29 43 38 -5
P3 4 65 49 42 -7
P4 2 15 48 50 +2

Key finding: Results are mixed but trending negative (3 of 4 participants showed decreased connection to nature). The post-study CNS survey was administered at the end of the academic semester, which may have impacted participants' baseline sense of connection to nature independent of the intervention.

Measurement limitations: CNS data is qualitatively noisier than the immediate pre/post mood measures and shows a trending lower pattern in week 3. Interpret with caution.

Important: CNS should be interpreted cautiously given timing issues. Mood and AWE data (measured immediately before/after sessions) are more reliable indicators of intervention impact.

Discussion

Qualitative insights:

  1. Pattern recognition demonstrated: Untrained participants identified possible earthquakes, frequency glides, and volcanic tremor through sound alone.
  2. High engagement: 283 features annotated, 36% with substantive scientific notes - exceeds typical citizen science rates.

Individual Variation: The Story of Participant 4

Participant 4 consistently displayed positive shifts from engagement with audified volcanic data:

  • CNS increased (+2 points, only participant)
  • High quality annotations: 100% of features have detailed notes
    • "transition during/after which relatively rapid irregular clicks start occurring, visible as faint vertical lines"
    • "series of distinct short clicks followed after varying delays by more sustained whooshing sounds"
  • Best mood shifts: Showed positive changes in multiple sessions
  • Highest AWE-SF among multi-session participants

This participant demonstrates one pattern of response combining cognitive engagement with positive affective outcomes. N=6 reveals individual variation that would be lost in larger aggregate studies.
