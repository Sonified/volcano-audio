Study Overview

What we did: Developed a web-based platform that transforms volcanic seismic data into audible sound. Participants (N=6) listened to 24 hours of volcanic data, marked interesting features, and completed pre/post mood surveys plus awe and nature connectedness scales.

Study Design

  • N = 6 real participants (after excluding test accounts)
  • 17 complete sessions across 3 weeks (all with Pre+Post mood data)
  • Data source: USGS volcanic seismic station data (Nov-Dec 2025)
  • Measures:
    • Pre/Post mood (6 items, 1-5 scale) - administered immediately before and after each session
    • AWE-SF (12 items, 1-7 scale) - administered immediately after each session
    • CNS (14 items, 1-5 scale) - Pre at intake, Post before final session (not after completion)
    • Feature annotations - marked during listening sessions

Methodological Note on Timing

Mood and AWE data (measured directly before/after each intervention)

CNS data = qualitatively noisier, trending lower in week 3

Outcomes

Volcano sonification significantly enhanced focus (p=0.0041, Δ=+0.41) - a clear cognitive benefit measured immediately before and after each session. This represents increased concentration and attention during the listening experience.

Calm also showed a strong positive trend approaching significance (p=0.096, Δ=+0.29), suggesting the platform promotes relaxation alongside focus. Connected to nature improved (+0.24) and anxiety decreased (-0.18), though these changes were not statistically significant. The combination of increased focus with improved calm creates a "relaxed focus" state - enhanced concentration without tension or anxiety.

283 features identified across 6 participants:

Key observation: P3 identified tremor through sound despite not seeing it visually: "I don't 'see' this feature, but I hear a continuous rumbling that's not always there." This validates auditory perception as complementary to visual analysis.

Annotation quality:

  • 36% of features have substantive notes (40 with scientific terminology, 12 with metaphorical descriptions)
  • This exceeds typical citizen science annotation rates

What This Means

Key Findings Summary

Significant effects (p < 0.05):

  1. Focus increased (p=0.0041, Δ=+0.41) - clear cognitive benefit measured immediately before/after each session

Trending effects (p < 0.10, require larger N for significance):

  1. Calm increased (p=0.096, Δ=+0.29) - approaching significance, positive relaxation trend
  2. Energized decreased (p=0.083, Δ=-0.35) - negative trend, may reflect relaxation vs. stimulation trade-off

Positive trends (not significant, consistent direction):

  1. Connected improved (Δ=+0.24) - positive trend in feeling connected
  2. Anxiety decreased (Δ=-0.18) - positive trend in reduced nervousness
  3. Wonder stable (Δ=+0.06) - minimal change

Qualitative findings (high-quality behavioral data):

  1. Pattern recognition demonstrated: Untrained participants identified possible earthquakes, frequency glides, and volcanic tremor through sound alone
  2. High engagement: 283 features annotated, 36% with substantive scientific notes - exceeds typical citizen science rates

Awe and nature connectedness:

  1. Low emotional awe (2.75/7, measured post-session) - BUT high cognitive engagement (Accommodation 4.15/7)
    • Platform creates intellectual challenge, not emotional overwhelm
  2. CNS inconclusive but trending lower in week 3

Areas Requiring Larger Sample

Several positive trends require N>50 to reach statistical significance:

  • Calm (p=0.096) - very close to significance, clear positive trend
  • Connected (positive direction, moderate effect size)
  • Anxiety reduction (consistent with "relaxed focus" pattern)

Current N=6 (17 sessions) provides adequate power for large effects (like Focus) but is underpowered for medium effects.

Data Quality Hierarchy

Most Reliable

  1. Mood changes (pre/post each session) → ✅ Focus significantly increased
  2. AWE-SF (immediately post-session) → ✅ Low awe, high cognitive engagement
  3. Feature annotations (during sessions) → ✅ Pattern recognition demonstrated

Less Reliable

  1. CNS (trending lower in week 3) → ❓ Mixed, treat with caution

Individual Variation: The P4 Story

P4 is the ideal outcome:

  • CNS increased (+2 points, only participant)
  • High quality annotations: 100% of features have detailed notes
    • "transition during/after which relatively rapid irregular clicks start occurring, visible as faint vertical lines"
    • "series of distinct short clicks followed after varying delays by more sustained whooshing sounds"
  • Best mood shifts: Showed positive changes in multiple sessions
  • Highest AWE-SF among multi-session participants

P4 demonstrates it's possible to achieve both cognitive engagement AND positive outcomes. N=6 reveals this variation that would be lost in larger aggregate studies.
